NFC Card Tester: what it does and how to use it

This Windows app talks to your contactless card the way a phone app does, and it can load applets onto the card the way PyApduTool does. It is meant for one card: the electrical_electrometer ("EMG") applet on a Java Card. Read this page once and you will know every screen.

What it can and cannot tell you

The app follows the written rules of iOS Core NFC and Android IsoDep, and sends real commands to your card through a PC reader. That answers most questions without a phone, a Mac or a developer account.

Worth knowing

  • It CAN tell you: whether a phone app would find the card, whether the applet answers every command correctly, what is stored on the card, and what is loaded on it.
  • It CANNOT tell you: how well the phone's antenna reaches the card, how fast the phone's NFC chip is, or how a particular iOS or Android version behaves. A real phone app is still the final proof.
  • A PC reader is not a phone. Where the two differ, the app says so rather than hiding it.

Before you start

Three things, every time.

How to use it

  1. Put the card on the contactless reader.
  2. If PyApduTool (or any other card tool) is open, click Disconnect there. A reader can only be held by one program at a time.
  3. Start the app and click Connect, the green button at the top left. The reader is chosen for you; the bar then shows the card's ATR.
  4. When you are done, click Disconnect so other programs can use the reader again.

Worth knowing

  • No reader? Choose "Simulated card" in the list. It runs your applet's logic in software, so every screen works without hardware.
  • Until you click Connect, the buttons that talk to the card stay switched off.

iPhone or Android

The switch at the top right decides which phone's rules the app follows. It changes what is allowed, not just the wording.

PhoneWhat it does
iPhone (Core NFC)Finds the card only by the AIDs in the app's list, in order. The app may only SELECT those AIDs; payment AIDs are always blocked. A session ends after 60 seconds. Needs a paid Apple Developer account on a real iPhone.
Android (IsoDep)Finds any ISO-DEP card with no AID list. Sends whatever bytes you give it. No session limit. Any app with the NFC permission works, so no developer account is needed.

Worth knowing

  • Each phone keeps its own results. Switching to Android never shows you the iPhone's results, and switching back brings the iPhone's results again.
  • The AID box at the top right decides which applet every screen selects: the first AID in it. On an iPhone the whole list is what an app would put in its Info.plist, tried in order.
  • Burning an applet puts its AID in that box by itself, so the check, the console, the read and the full test all follow the applet that is really on the card.

Page 1 - Check

One click answers the question: would a phone app find this card and use the applet?

How to use it

  1. Click "Check this card like an iPhone" (or Android).
  2. Read the verdict banner: YES, YES with warnings, or NO.
  3. The steps underneath show exactly what the phone did and what the card answered.

Worth knowing

  • It is read-only: nothing on the card is changed.
  • For your card the iPhone answer is YES with one warning, because the AID starts with 4 rather than A, D or F. Apple has no written rule against it, but one developer saw iOS refuse such an AID.

Page 2 - APDU console

Type one command in hex and send it the way the chosen phone would send it.

How to use it

  1. Type the command, for example 00 A4 00 00 02 30 40. Spaces are optional.
  2. Under the box the app explains what you typed: the class, instruction, offset, how many bytes it asks for.
  3. Press Enter or click Send. The answer shows the status word, what it means, and the data.

Worth knowing

  • The quick buttons fill the box with common commands. None of them writes to the card.
  • Up and Down in the box bring back earlier commands.
  • Under each answer the app names the exact check inside the applet that produced it, from the error map.
  • On iPhone rules, a command the phone would refuse is shown as BLOCKED and never reaches the card.

Page 3 - Read and write card

Reads the whole file off the card and shows it, byte for byte, or writes bytes into it. The switch at the top of the page chooses Read card or Write card.

How to use it

  1. The switch at the top of the page has three modes: Read card, Write card, and Block editor.
  2. These three modes talk to whichever card is chosen on the Card profiles page (a tile on Home). EMG is the default, so nothing changes for the EMG card.
  3. Read card: click "Read whole card" and watch the block map fill in, 128 blocks of 256 bytes. When it finishes, the data report opens in your browser by itself.
  4. Write card: choose what to write on the left: hex bytes at an offset, a .bin file, or a fill (one byte over a range). The bytes panel shows them before anything is sent.
  5. Block editor: type a start offset (in hex, 0000-7FFF) and how many 16-byte blocks to read, click "Read blocks", edit any byte in the grid, then "Write changes" sends only the blocks you changed back to the card. The offset is lined up to a 16-byte block, so 7FF0 reads the last block (7FF0-7FFF). The parameters the card carries (card ID, company, versions) show above when offset 0 is in the range.
  6. Click "Write to card" and confirm. The page then shows every byte as changed, already the same, or wrong on read-back.
  7. Changed your mind? "Undo last write" puts back what was there.

Worth knowing

  • Read card is OK only at exactly 32768 bytes. Fewer or more is an exception, and the page tells you which parts could not be read and why.
  • It always shows the data it managed to read first, and the problem second. A bad block does not stop the rest.
  • Bytes that could not be read appear as a red -- both on the page and in the report.
  • "Save .bin" writes a 32768-byte file where the position in the file is the offset on the card. Write card can put such a file back.
  • "Save .txt" writes the same bytes as one line of hex, the form the Elsewedy Smart Card Utility reads and writes, so a card read here opens there. Write card reads both kinds, so a *_RFID_Card_*.txt made by those tools can be written from here.
  • After a read, "What the card says about itself" shows the meter card's own first 64 bytes: the card ID, the company, the region and the versions. A card without that block (a fresh applet, a card of 00) simply does not show the row.
  • Choosing a firmware or bootloader card file in Write card says what it is before you write it: its version, how many frames it holds and which part of the whole firmware they are.
  • Before Write card changes anything, it reads what is there and saves it as card_backup_<range>.bin. Undo only goes to the card the write was made on, so it cannot spoil another card.
  • A write is only OK when reading it back gives exactly the bytes that were written.
  • This tool is not only for the EMG card. On the Card profiles page (a Home tile) you pick or add a profile — a card's AID(s), how to select its file, how big it is, and the READ and UPDATE commands — and Read, Write and Block editor follow it. Offsets then run 0000 to the profile's last byte, not always 7FFF. The console and the Burner work with any card and do not use the profile.

Page 4 - Card Unit Test

The 40 checks of card_test.ps1, 15 extra checks, and the card size: 59 results in all.

How to use it

  1. Click "Run Card Unit Test" and confirm.
  2. Tap 1 runs the checks and the card size; then the saved bytes are written back; then tap 2 checks what survives a power cycle.
  3. Use the filters (All, Not PASS, 40 checks, Extra) to find what you care about.
  4. "HTML report" saves everything as a web page; "Save CSV" gives you a spreadsheet.

Worth knowing

  • It writes to the card, but only inside 0000-003F, 0100-01FE and 7FC0-7FFF. Those areas are saved first and written back at the end. If they cannot be saved, nothing is written at all.
  • Every check runs, even after a failure, so a card that answers wrongly still shows all its answers.
  • The TYPE column names the applet check behind each answer, or marks it as an answer only the OLD applet gives.
  • Card size passes only at 32768 bytes.

Page 5 - Run all tests

One button that does everything else on this list and ends with a single answer: is this card ready?

How to use it

  1. Click "Run all tests" and confirm.
  2. Watch the stages: the check, the card read and the card unit test as an iPhone, then the same as an Android, then the production checks.
  3. Read the verdict at the top, and the report that opens in your browser.

Worth knowing

  • The read-only runs come first on each phone and the card unit test last, so a card that is already wrong is found before anything is written to it.
  • The production checks are the ones no other screen makes: that the card picks the applet by itself after a power-up, that the card ID is usable and stays the same over five power-ups, that an app can select the applet by its AID, and that the whole data file is addressable.
  • The verdict is NOT READY if anything failed, READY with warnings if only warnings, READY if nothing at all.
  • Running it on the simulated card says so next to the verdict: a READY from the simulator says nothing about your hardware.

Page 6 - Burner

The card manager: load an applet onto the card, and read, select or delete what is already there. This is the only page that changes which code is on the card.

How to use it

  1. Type the card's three keys in the boxes, click "Default test key" for the keys in PyApduTool's config.ini, or click "From a config.ini" to take them out of another one. Click "Save keys" and the app will remember them next time.
  2. Click "Open channel". The card proves it has the same keys and then lists everything on it.
  3. To load an applet: choose the .cap file, check the package AID and the instance AID it shows, then click "Load and install" and confirm. The instance AID box is what the applet will answer to, so change it there if you want a different one; the AID box at the top then points at it.
  4. The arrow button next to an applet selects it the way an app would. The bin button deletes a package or an applet.
  5. To burn a whole tray: set the CAP and the settings once, then click "Auto load" instead of "Load and install". From there you only swap cards.

Worth knowing

  • If a card was burned before, its applet may already use that AID, or another applet holds "Default selected", and the install is refused. Tick "Force" (under the settings) and the tool deletes that applet — and any default-selected one — first, then installs the new one; their data is lost. Auto load follows the Force tick too.
  • Only three keys are ever needed. Nothing else about the card manager comes from a file.
  • The keys never go to the card and are never written to a report, a log or the APDU list: only cryptograms travel. The boxes stay hidden until you click the eye.
  • A wrong key set is caught before the card is asked to accept it, so it does not count against the card's limit on failed attempts.
  • Only ONE applet on a card can be "Default selected", the one the card picks after every power-up. If another applet already holds it, the app tells you which one before sending anything.
  • Whatever else the card would refuse (the package already there, an AID already taken) is also said before anything is sent, so a load never stops halfway with the old package gone.
  • "Default test key" takes the test card's keys from PyApduTool's config.ini. The simulated card holds the same keys, so they open both. A burn on the simulated card gives a new card ID and an empty file, as on the real card.
  • "Auto load" burns the same CAP, with the same settings, onto one card after another. Press it once and confirm; after that put a card on, wait for "OK finished for 1", take it off and put the next one on. The panel counts them and names every card.
  • Auto load needs the three keys and a CAP, but not an open channel: it opens one for every card by itself. It keeps going until you press "Stop auto load" — it does not stop by itself, even if some cards fail. The card being burned is always finished first.
  • A card has to leave the reader before the next one counts, so the same card is never burned twice.
  • At the end of the run a report opens by itself: how many cards were burned and three numbers for every card, with the IDs again on their own so they can be pasted into a list. "Open report" brings it back.
  • Which number names a card? The **chip ID** is burned in when the card is made and never changes, even after the card is burned again: that is the one to keep. The **card ID** (GET UID 80 08) is made afresh by every install, so it names the installation. The **reader UID** is random at every tap on this card, so it names only the tap.
  • Deleting a package also deletes the applets made from it, and their stored data. There is no undo.
  • Running a check, a read or the full test taps the card, and a tap resets it. That closes the card manager channel, so open it again before using the burner.

Page 7 - Log

Every command and answer, with the time it took.

Worth knowing

  • You do not have to come here to watch a test. The **live log** along the bottom of every page shows the same lines as they happen, and it opens by itself whenever a run starts.
  • Press the strip, or the arrow at its right, to keep it open or to put it away. Closed, it still shows the last line.
  • A line starting >> is what was sent and << is what the card answered, in green when the answer was 90 00 and red when it was not. The name in brackets is the check that sent it.
  • Search and filter the full log here, and save it as a text file.
  • Keys never appear in it, on purpose.

Page 10 - Card profiles

Which card the Read, Write and Block-editor screens talk to. The tool was built for the EMG electrometer applet, but a card profile moves everything card-specific here, so the same screens work with another card.

How to use it

  1. Pick a profile in the list on the left to make it the card the three screens use. EMG is the default.
  2. Click "New profile" to add one, or "Duplicate" a built-in to start from it.
  3. Fill in the fields on the right: a name, the applet AID(s), the SELECT-file APDU (empty if selecting the applet already opens the file), the card-ID APDU (empty if the card has none), the file size, and the READ/UPDATE class and instruction. "Save" keeps it and makes it the active card.

Worth knowing

  • A profile covers a card that keeps its data in one transparent binary file, read and written by offset, like EMG. MIFARE sector cards and record files are a different shape and are not covered.
  • The built-in profiles (EMG electrometer, Plain binary file) cannot be changed or deleted — duplicate one to make your own.
  • Your profiles are saved in %APPDATA%\NFC Card Tester\card_profiles.json and come back next time.
  • The console and the Burner work with any card and do not use the profile.

Page 11 - Make firmware card

Turns a meter firmware image into the set of RFID cards that write it to the meter. It never touches a reader; it makes the .txt (and .bin) files you then write to the card with the Burner or the Elsewedy utility.

How to use it

  1. Choose the meter firmware image (.bin) — the finished image your build produces. A 5-character version is filled in from the file name; check it matches the build.
  2. Click Make. The tool knows the SOGWAVE 1-phase recipe (the auth blocks 0x40 and 0xD0), so it splits the firmware into cards of 117 frames and writes them — no template needed.
  3. The card files land in Outputs_Files (hex text + .bin), named _RFID_Card_1, _2, ... Write them to the card in order to flash the meter.

Worth knowing

  • Each card carries the product's auth blocks — 0x40 (one value for the family) and 0xD0 (one per card frame-count) — copied from real cards, plus fresh frames and CRCs. No AES key is needed; the output is byte-for-byte what the Elsewedy utility makes.
  • For a firmware the built-in recipe does not cover (another product, or a size whose last card has a frame-count with no stored 0xD0), give a TEMPLATE set in step 2: pick one card of an existing set for that product and its headers are copied.
  • The firmware must be the finished meter image: a whole number of 256-byte pages carrying its own length and CRC tail in the last page.
  • Tested against a real EM120 set: the cards built from the firmware match the utility's files byte-for-byte.

Files the app writes

Everything is saved in the Outputs_Files folder next to the app and opens by itself. The app finds that folder from where it runs, so moving or checking out the tool folder keeps the files with it.

FileWhat it holds

Worth knowing

  • The card manager keys and your card profiles are kept separately in %APPDATA%\NFC Card Tester and never in these files.
  • If the app is somewhere it may not write, it falls back to Documents\iPhone NFC Tester rather than losing the file. The message after every save names the full path.

If something goes wrong

The usual causes, in the order worth checking.

What you seeWhat it usually means
Connect fails, "in use"Another program holds the reader. Click Disconnect in PyApduTool and try again.
The phone does not find the cardOn iPhone rules, one AID in the list must answer 90 00. Check the AID list box, and that the applet is installed and selected by default.
Everything answers 6D 00The card is running a different applet than you think. Open the Burner and look at what is actually installed.
The burner says 69 85 on install, or "an applet with that AID is already on the card"A card burned before. Tick "Force" to delete that applet (and any default-selected one) and install the new one, or delete it by hand first, or untick "Default selected".
Commands stop halfwayThe card was taken off the reader, or the iPhone's 60 seconds ran out. Tap again.
